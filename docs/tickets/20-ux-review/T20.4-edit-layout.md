# T20.4 Edit: the timeline in view at 1280×720
Phase 20 · Size M · Depends on: — · Findings: UX-03, UX-23 (guide question G1)
*Written 2026-11-11 (Claude Opus 5.5) from the T20.1 review.*

## Goal
At 1280×720, the smallest supported window, the Edit stage shows the
player, the toolbar on one row, and at least the ruler, Pictures and Voice
lanes without scrolling. At 1440×900 the Graphics lane is visible too.

## Why
The player is `65vh` tall, so the timeline starts at the fold at 1440×900.
At 1280×720 the toolbar wraps to two rows and no lane is visible.
Screenshots: `docs/ux-review/2026-11-11/j2-14-edit.png`, `s-06-edit.png`.
After Phase 17 the timeline is the main work surface. Camera tools also
show on projects without camera paragraphs, and the inspector's "Ask"
button has no explanation.

## Read first
- `static/js/timeline/core.js` (the shell markup and stage sizing);
- the `.tl-stage` and `.tl-main` CSS;
- `docs/design/studio-flow.md` §7 "07 Edit".

## Steps
1. **Size the player from the space left.** Its height is the viewport,
   minus the shell bars, the toolbar and a minimum track height (ruler,
   Pictures, Voice and Graphics, about 230 px). Keep its aspect ratio.
   Below 360 px tall the player shrinks rather than pushing the track off
   screen.
2. **Fit the toolbar on one row.** Keep speed, Ripple/Roll and Shortcuts.
   Move "loop picture", "draw all", "Auto-edit · all camera paragraphs" and
   "Undo auto-edit" into a "More" menu button. Show the camera items only
   when a paragraph is on camera.
3. **Label Ask.** Rename it "Ask Claude about this", with a tooltip naming
   what it sends.
4. **Player placeholder copy.** Use sentence case: "The opening slate isn't
   drawn yet. Press play to draw it."
5. Add snapshot fixtures at 1280×720 and 1440×900.

## Acceptance
- At 1280×720: no wrapped toolbar, and the ruler, Pictures and Voice lanes
  are visible without scrolling.
- At 1440×900 the Graphics lane is visible too.
- Nothing in the T17 timeline gestures regresses: scrub, section drag,
  trims, + inserts and graphics.
