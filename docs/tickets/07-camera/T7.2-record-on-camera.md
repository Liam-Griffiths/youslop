# T7.2 Record on camera in the Record tab
Phase 7 · Size M · Depends on: T7.1

## Goal
The Record tab can record a paragraph on camera (picture and voice
together), the same way it records voice-over today: pick the paragraph,
read it from an on-screen prompter, stop, review, keep or retake. Footage
shot on a phone or camera can be dropped onto a paragraph instead.

## Why
Recording is where piece-to-camera succeeds or fails. The voice-over flow
already works (per paragraph, mic check, level meter, upload on stop); this
extends it to video rather than building a second recorder.

## Read first
- `static/app.js` (`static/js/tabs/record.js` after T4.1): `ensureMic`
  (~5309-5344), `toggleRec` (~5452: `MediaRecorder` with an audio-only mime
  list, chunks, `uploadTake` on stop), the level meter (`dbOf`, `meterPos`
  ~5393), the mic check panel.
- `app/desktop.py`: the per-platform **microphone** permission patches
  (macOS proven; Linux and Windows written but unverified) and
  `corkbored.spec`'s `NSMicrophoneUsageDescription`.
- T7.1/T7.3's upload endpoint: `app/main.py:upload_camera` and
  `app/camera.py:process_camera_take` (separate silent MP4 and cleaned WAV).

## Steps
1. **Mode switch** in the Record tab: "Voice" | "On camera". The choice is
   remembered per project; a paragraph's existing `on_camera` preselects it.
2. **Camera stream.** `getUserMedia({audio: <same constraints as the mic
   path>, video: {width: {ideal: 1920}, height: {ideal: 1080}, frameRate: {ideal: 30}}})`.
   A camera picker and a mic picker (`enumerateDevices`), remembered.
   Show the live preview **mirrored** (CSS `transform: scaleX(-1)`) but
   record unmirrored.
3. **Framing guides** over the preview: thirds, and a headroom line. Show
   the camera's actual resolution and frame rate (from
   `track.getSettings()`) and warn under 720p.
4. **Prompter.** The paragraph's narration in large type in a band at the
   **top** of the preview, close to the lens, so eyes stay near the camera.
   Font size and scroll speed are adjustable; it scrolls only while
   recording. A three-second countdown before recording starts.
5. **Recording.** `MediaRecorder` with the first supported of
   `video/webm;codecs=vp9,opus`, `video/webm;codecs=vp8,opus`,
   `video/mp4` (WebKit). `videoBitsPerSecond: 8_000_000`. Upload on stop to
   T7.1's endpoint with a progress bar (files are large: about 1 MB a
   second). The level meter keeps working during recording.
6. **Review.** After processing, play the silent camera MP4 and its separate cleaned WAV
   together in place, synchronising playback, seeking, pauses and end time. Keep (default) or Retake. Earlier takes are listed under
   "Earlier takes" (T4.9) with "use this".
7. **Drop a file.** A file input on each paragraph accepts video from
   another device and posts it to the same endpoint.
8. **Desktop permissions.** Extend each platform patch from microphone to
   camera: add `NSCameraUsageDescription` to the spec, request
   `AVMediaTypeVideo` as well as audio on macOS, allow
   `CoreWebView2PermissionKind.Camera` on Windows, and on WebKitGTK the same
   `UserMediaPermissionRequest` already covers video (check
   `request.props.is_for_video_device`). Rename the functions
   `_patch_*_media`.

## Acceptance
- In a system browser (Chromium) on this machine: record a paragraph on
  camera with the prompter, review it, keep it; it appears on the paragraph
  with the picture in sync.
- In the desktop window (`./run.sh`, PyWebView on WebKitGTK): the camera
  permission is granted without a dialog, and recording works. If WebKitGTK
  cannot record video, record that in the Log with the console error and
  fall back to "open in browser" for camera recording.
- A phone clip dropped onto a paragraph becomes its camera take.

## Don't
- No editing of the take here (trims and jump cuts are T7.4).
- Do not record the mirrored image.
- Never delete an earlier take on retake.

## Implementer notes
*Written 2026-10-26.*

- The Linux and Windows media permission code in `desktop.py` has **never
  been run** (its own docstring says so). Expect to debug it here, on
  Linux first since that is the owner's machine. Its docstring also says the
  Record tab works in any system browser pointed at the same URL; keep that
  as the fallback.
- WebKit's `MediaRecorder` produces MP4 (H.264/AAC), Chromium's produces
  WebM. T7.1's processing reads either through ffmpeg, so the front end only
  needs the mime-type list.
- Recording at 8 Mbit/s for a 60-second paragraph is about 60 MB. Upload
  with `XMLHttpRequest` and a `FormData` body to expose real upload progress
  (`fetch` does not expose FormData upload progress); the server is
  local, so this is fast. Add a size guard (say 2 GB) with a clear message.
- Keep the voice-over code path untouched; branch on the mode at the top of
  the record function and share the meter, timer and upload progress.
