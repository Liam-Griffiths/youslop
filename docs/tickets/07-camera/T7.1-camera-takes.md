# T7.1 Camera takes in the data model
Phase 7 · Size M · Depends on: T0.5, T4.9

## Goal
A paragraph can be **on camera**: its take is a video recording with the
voice in it. The audio is cleaned by the same voice chain as a mic take, the
picture is cut at exactly the same points, and both are stored without ever
deleting an earlier take.

## Why
Piece-to-camera is a kind of video Corkbored must support, both as sections
inside a video and as whole talking-head videos (owner, 2026-10-26). Today
the only camera support is `Project.presenter`: one uploaded clip placed at
the start, after the intro or at the end. A camera take per paragraph lets
the existing timing model (take duration plus the paragraph gap) drive
camera footage exactly as it drives voice-over.

## Read first
- `app/schema.py`: `Presenter` (~324), `Slide.take` / `takes`, `MIC_TRACK`
  (~772), `Project.set_take`, `use_track`, `sync_takes`, `take_track`.
- `app/audio.py`: `process_take` (~322: returns `trimmed = [start, end]`,
  the span the voice chain keeps), `speech_span`, `voice_chain` (its
  `atrim=start:end` is that same span).
- `app/main.py`: `upload_take` (~1888), `upload_presenter` (~2408: probes

- T4.9 (keep every take): `next_take_path`, `Slide.history`.

## Steps
1. **Schema.** `CAMERA_TRACK = "camera"` beside `MIC_TRACK`. A camera take is
   a take dict with extra keys:
   ```python
   {"path": "takes/camera/<sid>.wav",        # cleaned voice, like any take
    "video": "takes/camera/<sid>.mp4",       # the picture, cut to the same span
    "raw_path": "raw/<sid>.webm",            # the upload, never touched again
    "trimmed": [start, end],                 # seconds into the raw file
    "tail": 1.6,                             # seconds of raw picture kept after `end`
    "width": 1920, "height": 1080, "fps": 30,
    "duration": 7.42, "words": [...], "kind": "camera"}
   ```
   `Slide.on_camera: bool = False`. When true, the slide's active take is
   `takes["camera"]` regardless of `Project.voice_track`; when false, the
   project's voice track as today. Put the rule in one place,
   `Project.active_take(slide)`, and make `set_take` / `sync_takes` use it.
2. **Processing**, `app/camera.py`:
   ```python
   def process_camera_take(raw: Path, out_wav: Path, out_mp4: Path,
                           voice: Voice | None) -> dict:
       """Clean the voice with audio.process_take, then cut the picture to the
       same span, plus up to TAIL_S of picture after it for the paragraph gap."""
   ```
   - Extract the audio to a temp WAV, run `audio.process_take` on it (so mic
     and camera voices sound the same), read `trimmed`.
   - Cut the video from `trimmed[0]` to `trimmed[1] + tail`, where
     `tail = min(TAIL_S, raw_duration - trimmed[1])` and `TAIL_S = 2.5`.
     Normalise to an editing-friendly intermediate: constant 30 fps
     (`fps=30`), H.264 `-crf 16 -preset veryfast -g 30` (a keyframe every
     second, so later cuts are cheap), `yuv420p`, no audio track (the voice
     lives in the WAV).
   - Probe and store width, height, fps; refuse anything under 640 px wide.
3. **Endpoints.** `POST /api/projects/{pid}/slides/{sid}/camera` accepts a
   recording or a file. It writes the raw upload with `next_raw_path`, the
   outputs with `next_take_path` (T4.9), sets `on_camera = True`, and
   retires the previous camera take to `history`. `PATCH` on the slide
   accepts `on_camera` (switching back to voice-over keeps both takes).
4. **Storyboard and timeline.** `storyboard.build` paragraphs gain
   `on_camera` and `take.video`; `render.timeline()` paragraphs gain
   `on_camera`, so the Timeline can draw camera paragraphs differently.
5. **Tests** (synthetic media from ffmpeg `lavfi`: `testsrc2` picture plus
   a tone with silence either side): the WAV and MP4 have the same duration
   to within one frame plus `tail`; `trimmed` matches `process_take`'s; a
   second upload keeps the first in `history`; `active_take` returns the
   camera take when `on_camera` and the voice-track take when not; an old
   project without these keys loads unchanged.

## Acceptance
- Uploading a short clip of someone speaking to a paragraph produces a
  cleaned WAV and a cut MP4 whose first word is in sync (check by eye at
  quarter speed).
- Every earlier take is still on disk and listed in `history`.

## Implementer notes
*Written 2026-10-26 after reading the code.*

- `process_take` already returns `trimmed = [span.start, span.end]` and the
  voice chain's `atrim` uses exactly that span (`audio.py` ~188 and ~245),
  so cutting the picture at `trimmed` is frame-accurate sync by
  construction. Do not re-detect speech on the picture side.
- Cut the picture with `-ss` **after** `-i` (accurate seek), not before:
  `ffmpeg -i raw.webm -ss {start} -t {dur} -vf fps=30 ...`. A fast seek
  before `-i` snaps to a keyframe and puts the picture out of sync by up to
  a second on browser recordings.
- Browser recordings are variable frame rate and their audio and video
  start times can differ by tens of ms. Read both stream start times with
  `ffprobe -show_entries stream=codec_type,start_time` and shift the video
  cut by the difference. Log it; if it is over 100 ms, flag the take.
- Why `tail`: the paragraph gap after a camera take still needs picture.
  The camera was still rolling after the last word, so keep those real
  frames; T7.3 freezes the last frame only if the tail runs out.
- `Project.voice_track` stays the project's voice-over voice. `on_camera`
  is per paragraph, which is what makes mixed videos work (camera intro,
  voice-over middle, camera sign-off).
