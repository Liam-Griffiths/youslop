# T18.1 Narration voice: rewrite the rules, and add a style lint
Phase 18 · Size M · Depends on: —

## Goal
Generated scripts read like a person talking: varied sentence lengths, no
reflexive rule-of-three, no punchy fragment runs, no hype or foreshadowing
teasers. An offline lint measures it, so the change can be checked without
taste arguments.

## Why
See the Phase 18 README: the owner finds the current voice off-putting, and
our own prompt asks for the staccato ("Short declarative sentences. One idea
per sentence.").

## Read first
- `app/script_gen.py`: `NARRATOR_SYSTEM`, `_outline_prompt`, `_write_section`,
  `_hook_for_section`, `sponsor_prompt`, `ADAPTED_RULES`, `CAMERA_DELIVERY`,

- `app/check.py`: the finding shape (`_finding`) and how style findings

- `tests/unit/test_prompts.py`.
- Memory rule for 3D, which applies here too: rules must be general, not tuned
  to one example.

## Steps
1. **Rewrite the voice rules in `NARRATOR_SYSTEM`.** Keep it compact, since
   8B local models read it on every call. Keep the facts and filler
   discipline. Replace the sentence rule with:
   - **Sentence variety:** mix lengths. Most sentences carry a full thought,
     often with a clause of detail. Use a short sentence rarely, only when the
     moment earns it, and never two in a row for effect.
   - **No reflexive triplets:** don't group things in threes, whether
     adjectives, clauses or fragments, unless the facts have exactly three.
     Don't end paragraphs on a punchline fragment.
   - **No teasers or hype:** no foreshadowing ("it was only the beginning",
     "he had no idea"), no "here's the thing", "the inside scoop", "but here's
     where it gets interesting", "spoiler", and no exclamation marks.
     Transitions follow from cause, time or contrast.
   - **Register:** a well-read person explaining to a smart friend. Wit comes
     through precise detail and understatement. Plain verbs. No "dive",
     "unpack", "journey", "game-changer", "testament to".
   - **One short contrast example** (bad, then good), written generically,
     not about any project topic:
     - Bad: "He built it. He tested it. He failed."
     - Good: "He spent most of 1891 building the thing in his brother's barn,
       and the first test, in front of two investors, ended with it on its side."
2. **Apply the same voice everywhere narration is written:** section writing,
   the hook (an intriguing fact stated plainly, not a cliffhanger), sponsor
   reads, the adapted import mode (the faithful mode keeps the creator's own
   words), on-camera delivery, and rewrites. One shared constant, not copies.
3. **`app/style_lint.py`:** an offline lint over narration text, with
   deterministic, language-agnostic measures where possible:
   - fragment runs: two or more consecutive sentences of 5 words or fewer;
   - triplets: "X, Y, and Z" patterns of short items, and three consecutive
     short sentences with parallel openings;
   - teaser and hype phrases: a small curated pattern list, as families
     rather than single strings;
   - exclamation marks;
   - a low sentence-length spread across a paragraph (standard deviation
     below a threshold).
   Each hit reports the sentence and the reason. Thresholds live in one place.
4. **Surface it** as P3 `script.style` findings in `check.run` (style
   category, with paragraph jump), plus `cli check`. Don't block renders.
5. Tests: lint unit tests on hand-written good and bad passages. The good
   passages must pass, including one deliberate short sentence. Prompt tests
   assert the new rules are present in every narration prompt and the old
   "Short declarative sentences" rule is gone.

## Don't
- Don't loosen fact discipline or the filler bans.
- Don't special-case words from the test topics.
- Don't make the prompt long. Small local models must still follow it, so
  prefer one example over many rules.

  - Paragraph 1 sentence lengths: 13, 9, 13, 13, 7, 11, 12.
  - Paragraph 3 sentence lengths: 7, 7, 10, 6, 6, 15, 13.
- After: 9 sentences, mean 25.0 words, 0 lint hits. Clearly more natural.
- The repo's hand-built Tube Mail script: 113 sentences, 7 hits (6 per 100).

The prompt change works, but **the lint does not detect the habit**. Every sentence is over 5 words and the spread is above 2.0, so before and after both score 0. Fixes:
1. Add a `short_rhythm` paragraph rule:
   - applies to paragraphs with at least 4 sentences;
   - flags when the mean sentence length is below `SHORT_MEAN` (about 14 words), **or** when at least 60 % of sentences are at most `SHORT_SENTENCE` (about 12 words);
   - anchors on the first sentence, with every index listed;
   - thresholds go in the constants block.

   Tune it so that:
   - both "before" paragraphs above are flagged (use them as test fixtures);
   - all three "after" paragraphs pass;
   - the existing good-passage tests still pass;
   - a single deliberate short sentence in an otherwise varied paragraph passes.
2. Add the teaser transition family "stranger still", "even stranger", "but that's not all", "and then" opening a paragraph for effect: `^(?:stranger|weirder|even stranger) still\b` and similar. Keep it as a family regex, not single strings.
3. Count "hits per 100 sentences" with a small helper `rate(texts) -> float`, so the reviewer and T18.2 can compare drafts.
