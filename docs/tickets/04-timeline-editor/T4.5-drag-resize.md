# T4.5 Drag and resize timing
Phase 4 · Size M · Depends on: T4.4

## Goal
Cue, title and gap items on the layer rows can be dragged (move) and, for
titles and chapter holds, resized at their duration edge, snapping to frame
boundaries and to word boundaries, and saving to the T4.2 fields.

## Why
This is the editing feature that stops re-rendering for a half-second
timing fix.

## Read first
- `static/js/timeline/layers.js` (T4.4) item geometry; `tlWireScrub` ~743 for
  how pointer events on the strip are handled today (reuse its pattern).
- `TL` time ↔ pixel helpers (`tlClock`, `tlStepFor`, zoom).
- `render.timeline()` paragraphs: `words` is a count; add `word_times` with
  absolute `start` / `end` from the take (T0.6), plus `spoken`; `fps`.
- T4.2 fields and `patch_slide`.

## Steps
1. `static/js/timeline/edit.js`: pointer down on an item starts a drag with
   `{sel, mode: move|resize, x0, t0, t1}`; pointer move computes a new time;
   pointer up saves. `Escape` during a drag cancels.
2. Snapping: candidates are frame boundaries (`1/fps`) and word starts and
   ends from `words` of the paragraph under the item; snap within 6 px; hold
   `Alt` to disable. Draw a thin vertical guide at the snap target.
3. Constraints: a cue or title stays within its paragraph `[0, spoken]`;
   a gap handle (drawn at the paragraph end) moves between `0` and `10` s
   and shifts everything after it visually during the drag (so the user
   sees the effect) without touching the server until drop.
4. Resize: the right edge of a title sets `title_hold`; the left edge of a
   chapter sets `chapter_hold` (its end is fixed at the preceding picture boundary)
   with a minimum of `last_line_min_s` from the frame spec for the final
   title, else 0.5 s.
   Essay captions have no per-caption timing fields and remain selection-only.
   Derived cues must be materialized from the server-provided effective cues
   before changing `at`. Chapters resize only; there is no chapter arrival field.
5. Save: one `PATCH` per drop, then refresh `timeline()` and redraw; the
   dragged item shows `src: "user"` styling (a small dot) afterwards.
   "Reset timing" in the inspector clears the field (`null`).
6. Tests: pure JS snapping is hard to unit-test here; instead write the
   snap function in a way that is trivially readable and add a Python test
   for the server-side clamps in `patch_slide` (`at` outside `[0, spoken]`
   is clamped, `gap_override` > 10 is clamped).

## Acceptance
- Drag a cue 0.4 s later on the test project; the proxy replays with the
  cue later; drag it back onto a word start and it snaps.
- Resizing a title below 0.5 s is refused visually (handle stops).

## Don't
- Paragraph starts and ends are not draggable. The voice is the clock.
- Do not save on every pointer move.

## Implementer notes
*Added 2026-10-26.*

- Pointer pattern to copy: `tlWireScrub()` (timeline.js ~743). Use
  `setPointerCapture` on pointerdown so the drag survives leaving the row.
- Pixel ↔ time is `x / TL.zoom` (items are placed at `left: t * TL.zoom`);
  account for the track's scroll offset
  (`track.getBoundingClientRect().left`).
- Keep `tlSnap` readable. Snap to the nearest word edge within 6 px first,
  then the nearest frame within 6 px; Alt disables both. Comparing word
  distance against the nearest frame would make word snapping nearly
  unreachable at ordinary zoom levels.
  Word times must be **absolute** (paragraph start + word start); convert
  back to paragraph-relative before saving `at`.
- Words are estimates on every take today (T0.6 notes), so word snapping
  will be approximate; frame snapping always works.
- Server clamps: `at` needs `spoken`, which is the take duration
  (`slide.take["duration"]`). Clamp in `edits.patch_slide_fields`.
