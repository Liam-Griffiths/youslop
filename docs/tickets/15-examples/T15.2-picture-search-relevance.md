# T15.2 Picture search relevance: subject-aware queries and a relevance gate
Phase 15 · Size M · Depends on: —
*Written 2026-11-08 (Claude Opus 5.5) after building the example video, "The Tube Mail".*

## Goal
Automatic picture gathering returns pictures of the paragraph's subject, or nothing. It never returns an off-topic picture that only shares a keyword.

## Why
For a script about the Paris and Berlin pneumatic post, gathering chose these for 20 of 20 paragraphs:
- photos of a 1901 Paris–Berlin car race ("Die Woche Autorennen Paris-Berlin", used 8 times);
- ESCP business-school campuses ("Escp-Berlin", "Escp-Torino", "Escp-Paris", "Escp-London");
- a garden statue;
- a Citroën 2CV rally car;
- a fishing boat.

"Topped up 19 thin slides from the subject pool" spread the car race across the video. Yet good public-domain material was one query away. Searching by hand for "poste pneumatique Paris", "Rohrpost Berlin" or "carte pneumatique" returned 6–10 public-domain hits each: the 1880s engravings of the network, the Bourse office, the steam plant, real petit bleu cards and Rohrpost envelopes.

## Read first
- `app/media/__init__.py`: `search`, `_ladder`, `gather`, `_top_up`, `_autopick`, `subject_query`.
- `app/media/providers.py` (Wikimedia, Openverse, LoC, Met, NYPL), `app/script_gen.py` (`visual_note` and query writing).

## Steps
1. **Subject terms.** Derive the video's subject terms once from the title, the research dossier and the outline. Here that means "pneumatic post", "poste pneumatique", "Rohrpost", "pneumatic tube" and "petit bleu". Include each language's own term where the dossier gives it.
2. **Paragraph queries.** Each paragraph's queries pair its specifics (a place, a year, an object) with at least one subject term. A paragraph about the Bourse becomes "Bourse Paris poste pneumatique" first, then plain "Palais de la Bourse 1866", and never "Paris Berlin" alone.
3. **Relevance gate.** Score each hit's title, description and categories against the subject terms and the paragraph's nouns. A hit sharing only a place name (for example "Paris" and "Berlin" with no subject term) is rejected for automatic choice. It stays visible in the picker, marked "weak match".
4. **Top-up.** `_top_up` reuses only gated hits, and never the same picture for more than 2 paragraphs. If there aren't enough, the paragraph stays without a picture and gets a "Needs a picture" finding. A wrong picture is worse than a gap.
5. **Licence preference.** Optionally (a project setting, default on for the bundled example and off otherwise) prefer public domain and CC0 over CC BY/BY-SA when relevance is equal.
6. **Photographable queries (added 2026-11-08 from the local-model runs, see
   Phase 16).** The prompt that writes picture queries asks for things a camera,
   painter or mapmaker could have recorded: people, places, objects,
   documents, maps. It gives examples of good and bad queries. Granite 4.1 8B
   wrote "airburst explosion energy release megatons TNT" and "scientific
   theories Tunguska meteoroid comet fragment"; several paragraphs got no
   picture at all. Add a local check that drops queries made mostly of
   abstract words (theory, energy, hypothesis, analysis, estimate) when
   another query for the paragraph is concrete.
7. **Fixture test.** Build a small hand-made fixture of provider-shaped hits: the named bad matches and good public-domain pneumatic-post engravings, cards and envelopes, with title, description and categories. The example media cache was not kept; do not fetch responses. The test asserts that no automatic pick has a title matching `Autorennen|Escp|Citroen|senneur|Skulptur`, and that at least 10 of 20 paragraphs get a gated, subject-matching picture.

## Acceptance

- Re-gathering "The Tube Mail" (reviewer, with network) picks subject pictures for most paragraphs and leaves the rest empty. No car races.

### Found in review (2026-11-12, Claude Opus 5.5)
The offline Tube Mail fixture passes, but the gate over-rejects on other subjects because `subject_terms` usually returns a single two-word phrase and `gate` needs that whole phrase. Checked directly:

| Title / outline subject | Terms | Hit | Now | Should be |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "Shackleton's Endurance" / "Ernest Shackleton and the crew of the Endurance, Imperial Trans-Antarctic Expedition" | `['ernest shackleton']` | "Endurance trapped in the pack ice, 1915" | weak | ok |
| | | "Frank Hurley photograph of the Endurance at night" | weak | ok |
| | | "Ice in Antarctica" | weak | weak |
| "The Tunguska event" / "The 1908 Tunguska explosion in Siberia" | `['tunguska explosion']` | "Tunguska event fallen trees 1927 Kulik expedition" | weak | ok |
| | | "Siberian forest" | weak | weak |
| "How the Hoover Dam was built" / "Building the Hoover Dam on the Colorado River, 1931-1936" | `['hoover dam']` | "Hoover Dam construction 1934" | ok | ok |
| | | "Colorado River" | weak | weak |

On a normal project this would leave most paragraphs without a picture, which is worse than the bug. Fix it like this:

1. **Distinctive single words as anchors.** Anchors include distinctive single words from the title and the outline subject, as well as phrases. Examples are "tunguska", "endurance", "shackleton", "hoover" and "pneumatic". Exclude stop words, generic words (history, story, event, building, crew, post, city, river, built, explosion, expedition) and **place names**.
2. **Place names.** They must never act as anchors alone; that keeps "Autorennen Paris-Berlin" and "Escp-Berlin" rejected. Use a small built-in list of countries, major cities and regions, plus any capitalised word the research dossier or outline uses as a location. Keep it general: the rule must hold for any subject, not just this example.
3. **Gate unchanged.** A hit still needs at least one anchor (a phrase or distinctive word). Place-only matches stay weak.
4. **Tests.** Add the three cases above as tests, with the expectations in the "Should be" column. Keep the Tube Mail fixture assertions passing.

### Found in review, round 2 (2026-11-12, Claude Opus 5.5)
The three cases now pass, but ordinary words have become anchors:

| Title / subject | Terms now |
|---|---|
| "The Tube Mail" / "How Paris and Berlin sent letters by air (the pneumatic post)" | `['berlin sent', 'pneumatic post', 'tube', 'sent', 'letters', 'air', 'pneumatic']` |
| "Lagos traffic" / "Why Lagos traffic jams are so bad" | `['lagos traffic', 'traffic', 'jams', 'bad']` |

A picture titled with "air" or "bad" would pass, which brings the original bug back. Also, "Poste pneumatique Paris gravure" is weak against "pneumatic".

1. **Single-word anchors need evidence.** A single word becomes an anchor only if one of these holds:
   - it's a proper noun, capitalised mid-sentence in the title, the outline subject or the research;
   - it appears in at least two of the title, the outline subject and the research;
   - it's a long word (8+ letters) that isn't on a common-words list.

   Ship a compact built-in list of common English words (a few hundred is enough) and exclude those as single-word anchors. "sent", "letters", "air", "bad" and "jams" must not anchor.
2. **No place names in phrases.** Two-word phrase anchors may not contain a place name ("berlin sent" goes) or a stop word.
3. **Stem matching across languages.** A single-word anchor of 7 or more letters also matches any word sharing its first 6 letters, so "pneumatic" matches "pneumatique" and "Rohrposten" matches "Rohrpost".
4. **Tests.** Add the two rows above:
   - Tube Mail with no research: "Poste pneumatique Paris gravure" is ok; the car race, Escp-Berlin and "Air France Paris" are weak.
   - Lagos: "Lagos at night" is weak; "Danfo bus in Lagos traffic" is ok if "traffic" anchors through the phrase "lagos traffic". Accept either result for it.
   - Keep every earlier test passing.
